## Student Safety & Emergency Plan
### Preparedness, Clarity, and Calm Response

**Effective: September 15, 2026 | Version 1.3**

## Purpose & Commitment

Lions Light Academy is committed to a safe, orderly, and Christ-centered learning environment. This plan defines clear roles and procedures so adults can respond calmly and consistently when something goes wrong.

It applies to all co-op days, activities on church property, and all off-site LLA events.

---

## Safety Philosophy

Safety is a primary priority at LLA, supported through clear expectations, supervision, orderly conduct, and defined authority. A structured environment protects students and makes real learning possible.

---

## Background Checks

**Every adult in a teaching or volunteer role completes a background check before serving, renewed every two years.**

This applies to teachers, co-teachers, volunteers, room leads, and any adult supervising students on a co-op day or a field trip. No exceptions, and no one begins serving while a check is pending.

---

## Supervision Standards

### General supervision

- Students remain in supervised areas unless explicitly directed by an adult
- Adults actively supervise during instruction, transitions, and group activities. Presence in a room is not supervision
- Room leads make sure students know where to go next and what is expected during transitions
- Unsupervised wandering is not permitted

### Students working outside a classroom

A student may be sent to finish required work in the hallway or lounge and rejoin the class when it's done. This is a routine instructional redirect, not discipline, and it does not change the supervision standard.

- **The adult in the main room supervises the hallway.**
- **A student uses the teachers lounge only when an adult is already in it.** If the lounge is empty, no student is in it either.
- No student is ever sent to a space where no adult can see them.

### Suggested supervision ratios

Practical targets for typical co-op activities. Adjust for activity risk, room size, and student needs.

| Age group | Target |
|---|---|
| Elementary | 1 adult per 8–10 students |
| Middle School | 1 adult per 10–12 students |
| High School | 1 adult per 12–15 students |
| Higher-risk activities — labs, tools, outdoors | Increase supervision as needed |

### Facility supervision

- Students use restrooms appropriately and return promptly to supervised areas
- Restroom use should not interfere with instruction or transitions
- Concerns about student safety or behavior in common areas are addressed by leadership

## Health & Medical

### Illness

**Stay home if contagious.** A student or adult who has been ill should be **24 hours symptom-free** before returning to a co-op day.

This protects families who are managing immune conditions, newborns, or elderly relatives at home, and it is the single most effective thing we do to keep the co-op running through winter.

### Allergies

- Families disclose allergies at enrollment, including severity and whether an EpiPen is carried
- **Nut-free snacks are encouraged**
- Where a student carries an EpiPen or inhaler, its location and use are known to the teachers and leaders on duty that day

### Parent responsibility

- Parents disclose relevant medical needs — allergies, asthma, seizures, and anything else we would need to act on
- Parents provide clear written instructions for emergency response needs where applicable
- Leadership may request updates as needs change

### Response, not administration

- **LLA does not administer routine medications.**
- LLA will respond to medical concerns and contact parents and emergency services as appropriate
- Emergency devices a student carries — EpiPen, inhaler — are a separate matter from routine medication, and adults on duty are told where they are and when to use them
- Where a student's needs require a dedicated plan, leadership works with the family to define a realistic response protocol

### Our medical response

**Damian Brockway and Ginny Brockway are licensed paramedics, and they are LLA's medical response team.**

- On a co-op day, medical situations go to Damian or Ginny immediately
- Any adult who finds an injured or ill student sends for them at once and stays with the student
- A first aid kit is kept on site and adults on duty are told where it is
- Emergency contacts for every enrolled student are kept accessible
- 911 is called whenever the situation warrants it. Having paramedics on site does not replace emergency services, and no one hesitates to call

## Emergency Types Covered

- Fire, smoke, evacuation
- Medical emergency
- Behavioral crisis posing a safety risk
- Facility concerns — power loss, unsafe conditions

---

## General Emergency Procedure

1. **Stabilize** — ensure immediate safety; reduce movement and noise as directed
2. **Lead** — the Incident Lead takes control, assigns tasks, and directs adults
3. **Communicate** — call 911 if needed; contact parents as appropriate
4. **Account** — take attendance and verify every student's location
5. **Document** — record what happened and what was done

---

## Fire & Evacuation

- Identify exits at the start of the year and know the route from every room you teach in
- Evacuate promptly and calmly when directed
- Teachers bring rosters or an attendance method if available
- Students remain with their group and follow adult instructions
- **The meeting point is the shed behind the building.** Everyone goes there, every time
- **Attendance is taken at the shed** and reported to the Incident Lead

**We do not run scheduled fire drills.** We walk students and adults through the procedure at the start of the year and revisit it as needed. Every student should be able to answer "where do we go" without being told.

---

## Severe Weather — We Cancel

**LLA does not hold co-op days in severe weather. We cancel.**

- **If the local public school district cancels, LLA cancels.** That is the simplest rule and it is the one to remember
- Leadership may also cancel independently when conditions warrant it
- Cancellation is communicated to families by phone, text, and the co-op's Google Chat and Slack spaces as early as possible

**If weather turns during a co-op day**, leadership ends the day early and parents are contacted to collect students. Students stay in supervised interior spaces until collected.

---

## Facility Safety

- A first aid kit is kept on site and adults on duty know where it is
- Exits and the route to the shed are identified at the start of the year
- Unsafe conditions are reported to leadership immediately rather than worked around

We meet on church property and do not conduct our own facility inspections. Anything that looks unsafe gets reported rather than managed around.

---

## Post-Incident Communication & Review

- Parents are notified following significant incidents, using clear and factual communication
- Leadership reviews incidents to improve procedures and reduce future risk
- Patterns are tracked where needed to support student safety and accountability

---

## How We Prepare

- Every teacher and volunteer is walked through this plan before they serve. It takes a few minutes and it is the whole training
- Students are walked through fire procedure, the shed, and who to find in a medical situation on the first co-op day of the year, and reminded as needed
- **We talk through procedures rather than running scheduled drills.** For a co-op our size, meeting in a building we share, a conversation everyone actually has beats a drill nobody schedules
- Feedback from adults on duty is welcome and is used to strengthen these procedures

---

## Shared Responsibility

Student safety is shared among leadership, teachers and volunteers, parents, and students. Following the expectations and procedures in this plan is how we protect the learning community.

Where this plan and any other LLA document appear to conflict on a safety matter, **this plan governs.**

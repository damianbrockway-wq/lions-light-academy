# Lions Light Academy
## Google Classroom — A Guide for Families
### Getting in, staying in, and the three days that matter

**Effective: September 2026 | Version 1.0**

> Ten minutes to set up. Two minutes, three times a week, after that.

---

## Why this matters

Co-op meets one day a week. Everything in between — the reading, the practice, the project that's due next Tuesday — lives in Google Classroom.

A student who never opens it isn't behind because the work was hard. They're behind because they never saw it.

That's the whole reason for this page.

---

## Step one — get the app

It's free, and it's the same app on both phones.

- **iPhone or iPad** — App Store, search **Google Classroom**
- **Android** — Google Play, search **Google Classroom**

The icon is a green chalkboard. The publisher is Google LLC — there are lookalikes, so check that.

No phone? It works in any web browser at **classroom.google.com**. Nothing is lost by using the website instead.

---

## Step two — sign in as the student

**This is the step that goes wrong most often, so it's worth slowing down for.**

Whatever Google account is signed in on that device is the account that joins the class. If your child taps the invite while your phone is signed in as you, **your** account joins the class — not theirs. The roster then has a parent on it and the student still can't see their work.

Before joining anything:

- Tap the round profile picture in the top right
- Check whose account it is
- If it's the wrong one, add or switch to the student's Google account

If your student doesn't have their own Google account yet, make one before you go further. Trying to share a parent account across two children is where this gets genuinely tangled.

---

## Step three — join the class

There are two ways in, and your teacher will use one of them. You don't get to pick — just follow whichever one you were given.

### If you were sent an invitation

**The invitation lives in email, and only in email.** Nothing appears inside the Classroom app until it's accepted.

1. Open the **email inbox** for the student's Google account
2. Find the message from Classroom — subject line names the class
3. Tap **Join**

**If you can't find it, check the spam folder.** Classroom invitations land there often enough that it's the first place to look, not the last.

### If you were given a class code

A short code, six or seven characters, from the teacher.

1. Open the Classroom app
2. Tap the **+** in the top right
3. Tap **Join class**
4. Type the code exactly as given, then tap **Join**

Sometimes you'll get a link instead of a code. Tapping the link does the same thing with less typing.

### One code per class

Your student may be enrolled in more than one LLA class, and **each one has its own code or its own invitation.** Joining one doesn't join the others. If a class seems to be missing, that's usually why.

---

## Step four — check it Wednesday, Friday, Monday

This is the part that actually matters, and it takes about two minutes each time.

Three checks a week, each doing a different job:

**Wednesday — see what was assigned.** The day after co-op, while it's still fresh. Open it together with your student and read what's due. Nothing has to be done yet. You're just looking.

**Friday — start it, and ask questions.** There's still a whole weekend. If something doesn't make sense, this is the day to email the teacher and actually get an answer before it's urgent.

**Monday — the night-before check.** Anything unfinished is still fixable tonight. Tuesday morning is too late, and a rushed job on the way to co-op isn't the point.

Put those three as a repeating reminder on your phone. It is easier to keep a rhythm than to remember a task.

---

## What to look at when you're in there

Open the class, then tap **Classwork** at the bottom.

The **Stream** is the announcement feed. It's useful, but assignments can scroll off it. **Classwork** is the real list, and it shows what's assigned, what's due, and what's been turned in.

When a piece of work is done, open it and tap **Turn in**. Work that's finished but never turned in looks exactly like work that was never done.

## If something looks wrong

**The class is empty, or isn't there at all.** The invitation was never accepted. It's sitting in an email inbox — check the student's mail and the spam folder.

**You're in, but it's the wrong account.** Sign out, sign in as the student, and join again. Tell us so we can remove the wrong one from the roster.

**The code doesn't work.** Codes get reset after everyone joins. Email us and we'll send a fresh invitation.

**Nothing's been posted yet.** That happens, especially early in the year. Check again Friday before assuming something is broken.

---

## One thing worth knowing

Our Code of Conduct treats **required work not completed for two class sessions in a row** as a Moderate concern — the level that carries a documented warning.

That's not meant as a threat. It's the reason this page exists. Almost every student who reaches that point got there by not seeing the work, not by refusing it.

**Three two-minute checks a week is how that never happens.**

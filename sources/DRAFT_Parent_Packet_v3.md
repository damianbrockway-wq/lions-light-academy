## How We Teach

Our curriculum is flexible, student-led, and inspired by Finland's model of education. We're constructionists — we believe students learn by building, making, and doing, not by absorbing.

**Guiding principles:** Experience First · Ask, Don't Tell · Faith Woven In · Whole Child Focus

Every lesson flows from **Faith → Learning → Reflection**. Instead of telling students how plants grow, we let them plant seeds, watch light and water do their work, and marvel at God's design through discovery.

We measure growth over grades. Learning shows up as curiosity, engagement, and transformation rather than a score.

**And we push.** Excellence here isn't perfectionism and it isn't competition — we never rank children against each other. We ask each student for their whole effort, measured against their own ceiling. A student should leave a class knowing an adult believed they could do hard things.

## What a Co-op Day Looks Like

**Co-op meets every Tuesday.** Students arrive at **8:45 a.m.** and are dismissed at **1:45 p.m.** The day opens with worship and devotions, everyone together, then runs class blocks across our three age tracks — Elementary, Middle School, and High School — with snack breaks, a whole-school lunch, and cleanup at the end.

**Block lengths differ by track**, because a nine-year-old and a sixteen-year-old do not concentrate for the same length of time. High School runs two 80-minute morning blocks and a 60-minute afternoon class. Middle School and Elementary run 45- and 60-minute blocks, and finish the day together in one combined class.

**The calendar on our website is the authoritative schedule** — it carries the actual dates, class blocks, and any changes. The Co-op Day Schedule in our Resources library shows the general shape of a day; the exact block times vary by class and by term, so check the calendar for what's actually happening.

## What Your Family Commits To

**Co-op meets every Tuesday, and every family teaches, co-teaches, or volunteers.** Lions Light runs on parents. There is no staff to fall back on, and consistent attendance is what makes the co-op stable enough to be worth attending.

Contributions can be teaching a class, assisting a lead teacher, supervising and covering transitions, helping with field trips, or administrative support.

**Every adult in a teaching or volunteer role completes a background check**, renewed every two years.

**There is no tuition.** We charge one supply fee per family, per year — **$200**, waived for families who lead a class. Every dollar goes back to the host church or to classroom supplies and equipment. Nothing is kept as profit and no one is paid from it. It is a participation fee, not a tax-deductible donation.

## What Your Student Commits To

**Learning is the student's work, and nobody can do it for them.** Teachers teach. Parents support. The student shows up ready, attempts the work, and stays engaged in the room.

Lions Light Academy is a co-op of active learners. A student who will not take part in learning cannot remain in the program. We say that plainly here so it is never a surprise later.

Our teachers know their students and will use judgment when something real is going on in a child's life. That judgment belongs to the teacher. Hard weeks happen and are met with grace, but a hard week is not a standing exemption from the work.

**On correction:** our approach is restorative. We correct in order to keep a student, not to build a case against them. Restoration is real and it is offered to every student — but it is not automatic. A student who has done damage owns what happened, makes repair, and meets the terms of a written plan before they are restored.

The Code of Conduct and the Escalation & Discipline Policy set all of this out in full. Both are signed at enrollment.

## A Note on Maine Requirements

**Lions Light Academy is a supplemental co-op. We are not your child's school of record.**

Each family remains responsible for its own Maine homeschool notification, assessment, and record-keeping. Enrolling here does not satisfy any state requirement.

Our Maine Homeschool Checklist and High School Credit Summary are in the Resources library to help, but the responsibility stays with you.

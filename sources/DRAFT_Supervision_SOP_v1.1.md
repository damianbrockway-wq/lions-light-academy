### Operational Clarity for Co-op Days

## Purpose

This SOP sets out how a co-op day actually runs — roles, transitions, room setup, and dismissal. Its job is student safety, orderly movement, consistent supervision, protected instructional time, and clear adult roles.

It applies to all teachers, volunteers, and supervising adults.

**This is the *how* document.** What behavior means and what happens next is set by the Escalation & Discipline Policy. What you are authorized to do is set by Teacher & Volunteer Expectations. Where any of them appear to conflict on a safety matter, the Student Safety & Emergency Plan governs.

---

## Adult Roles & Responsibilities

### Teacher — Instructional Lead

Each instructional space has one designated Teacher, who is the final authority in that room unless leadership intervenes.

- Instruction and lesson flow
- Classroom management
- Holding expectations
- Deciding when behavior is costing the class its instruction
- Reporting concerns to leadership

### Room Lead — Operational Support

Assigned to larger or higher-movement spaces.

- Support transitions and flow
- Assist with supervision during movement
- Help keep order in common areas
- Make sure students know where to go next
- Do not override a teacher's decisions in their room

### Volunteer / Helper

- Reinforce teacher direction
- Assist with supervision and logistics
- **Correct behavior and hold expectations in the moment, as any supervising adult may**
- Do not override the teacher's direction in an instructional space
- Report discipline matters to the teacher or leadership rather than resolving them alone

**On correcting.** Every supervising adult at LLA — teacher, room lead, or volunteer parent — is authorized to correct behavior immediately and to remove a student from an activity temporarily when safety or order requires it. You do not need permission first.

What belongs to leadership is what comes *after*: issuing warnings, notifying families, defining repair, and deciding restoration or removal. Act on what's in front of you, then hand it up.

## Supervision Standards

- Students remain in assigned, supervised spaces, except when stepped out to finish required work as described below
- Adults actively supervise at all times — presence in a room is not supervision
- Movement between spaces happens only when directed
- Unsupervised wandering is not permitted

**Supervision ratios** are set in the **Student Safety & Emergency Plan** and are not repeated here, so there is only ever one set of numbers to keep current.

---

## Students Working Outside a Classroom

A student who arrives without work the lesson depends on, or falls behind during class, may be sent to finish it in the hallway or lounge and rejoin when it's done.

**Supervision.** The adult in the main room supervises the hallway. A student uses the teachers lounge only when an adult is already in it — if the lounge is empty, no student goes there. No student is ever sent to a space where no adult can see them.

**This is a routine redirect, not discipline.** Nothing is recorded and it counts toward nothing. It protects the lesson for the students who came ready.

---

## Room Setup & Start of Class

- Materials prepared before students arrive
- Expectations stated clearly at the start
- Students accounted for
- Work required for the lesson verified
- Instruction begins promptly

---

## Transitions Between Classes

- Transitions are supervised and time-limited
- Students move directly to their next assigned space
- Teachers and Room Leads assist with flow
- Loitering and unnecessary delay are addressed immediately

Orderly transitions protect both safety and instructional time.

---

## Handling Disruptions During Class

- The teacher addresses it immediately
- Clear redirection or correction is given
- If it continues, the student may be removed from the activity temporarily
- Anything beyond that follows the Escalation & Discipline Policy

**Teachers are not expected to sacrifice instruction to manage repeated disruption alone.** Report it and leadership will carry it from there.

---

## Assignment Preparedness

- Teachers may require assigned work to be completed before instruction
- A student without required work may be stepped out to finish it and rejoin the class — a routine redirect, not a penalty, and not a limit on their participation
- **Required work not completed for two class sessions in a row is a Moderate concern**, raised by the teacher to leadership

Preparedness is what makes co-op instruction work. A lesson built on reading nobody did stalls for everyone in the room.

---

## End of Class & End of Day

- Students are dismissed in an orderly manner
- Account for students during transitions
- Communicate concerns to leadership promptly
- Students are not dismissed independently unless explicitly directed

---

## Escalation & Leadership Support

- Act first on what's in front of you, then report
- Escalate when a pattern emerges rather than waiting for it to become a crisis
- Leadership support is available and expected — asking for it is not a failure
- Emergencies follow the Student Safety & Emergency Plan

---

## Professional Conduct During Operations

- Remain calm and professional
- Use clear, respectful communication
- Support leadership decisions consistently, and raise disagreements privately through proper channels
- Address concerns through proper channels rather than in front of students
